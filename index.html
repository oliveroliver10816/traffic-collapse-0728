The plan

●

One correction first: we do not yet know if it's still spreading

I told you RetaFit had started falling. That came from 27–28 July, which Google has not finished counting. On the last complete day, 26 July, RetaFit had its best day ever — 37 clicks, 563 impressions. The 27th–29th finalise around 30 July, and that is the fork in this plan. Everything before then is defensive.

01

Stop all link building. Today.

Us

Not one of the 60 newest links has ever been crawled by Google — they are earning us nothing. But the fleet of near-duplicate accounts behind them (osanix / osanixx / osannix, seven Google Sites properties) is exactly the pattern that converts a clean record into a manual penalty.

We just confirmed all three sites are penalty-free. That's an asset worth protecting — right now we're risking it for zero return.

02

Rip out the dead-X popup and the back-button trap

Us

ozem-plus traps mobile visitors in a full-screen overlay whose ✕ does nothing — the close handler is never registered. All three sites also hijack the back button.

It didn't cause this — the site that has it is the one sitting at #1 in Italy. But it's a named deceptive pattern, a quality classifier has just been over these domains, and removing it is free.

03

Leave Italy and the Dutch mobile pages completely alone

Us

Italy is #1.4 and booked $80 on 27 July. Netherlands holds #2 on mobile. No edits, no new links, no experiments on either.

These are the only pages Google still trusts. Every change is a chance to lose the eligibility we've still got.

04

Re-measure on 30 July — this is the decision point

Us

When 27–29 July finalise we get one clean answer: has it bottomed out, or is it still narrowing?

I'm not going to spend your week rebuilding on domains that may still be sliding. Three days of waiting buys the difference between repairing these and replacing them.

If it has bottomed out

Rebuild breadth on these domains

We're eligible for ~17 searches instead of ~150. The work is earning that breadth back: real depth on the pages that survived, the commercial tail in Italy and Poland (“oz+ farmacia” is #1 and has never been harvested), and genuine citations rather than pastebin links. Slow, but these domains keep their age.

If it's still narrowing

Move the content, keep the domains alive

Two-week-old shells that Google has decided against don't argue their way back. We move the proven pages onto a domain with real age, leave these running as they are, and stop investing in them. Nothing gets deleted — dead domains can recover quietly at a later update.

What we change regardless

The three rules that would have stopped this being a total loss.

One durable domain, not a fresh shell per offer. The sites that took our slots are running our exact playbook — brand-review pages bolted onto real, aged pharmacy domains. Our content already beats theirs on Bing. The variable was never the page, it was the domain: theirs are years old, ours were 11 and 15 days old. Every new offer we launch on a new two-week-old domain is a coin flip on the next update.

Never let a site be 98% one brand word again. RetaFit is the only one still working, and it's the only one where the biggest query isn't our own brand — it's a third-party TV term. That page is thin, fast, and grew while everything around it died. More pages shaped like that, from day one, on every site.

Budget these as three-week assets. Osanix earned across roughly ten good days. If a build can't pay back inside three weeks, it shouldn't be built the way we've been building it. Right now we pay the cost of a long-term asset and get the lifespan of a campaign.

And one loose end worth closing: 177 test clicks across 41 different tags are sitting in our own reporting and distorted the first analysis. One reserved prefix from now on so they strip out with a single filter.
