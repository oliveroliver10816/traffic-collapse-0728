The affiliate network's own logs — a different company, a different database — show the same cliff on the same day: Osanix outbound clicks went 129 → 61 → 5. Two independent systems cannot invent the same drop. Both sites are now 82–96% below where they sat before any advertising ever started, so this is not simply a campaign ending.

What it isn't

The three things worth ruling out, and the evidence that rules them out.

✓

Not the noindex tag

All 59 live pages return index, follow. No blocking headers, clean robots.txt. Google's own inspector returns PASS · Submitted and indexed on every page and crawled osanix.shop successfully at 12:16 today. Only the legal pages and /go/ redirects carry noindex — deliberate, since launch, and never in the sitemap.

✓

Not a Google penalty

Google logged no ranking update in July — the last one ended 26 June, a month before. On the two days people reported turbulence, osanix was climbing to its best day ever. And ozem-plus held position 1.0–1.7 in Holland, Italy and Poland right through the crash, and booked a real $80 sale on the 27th. Penalties don't leave you at #1.

✓

Not something we shipped

ozem-plus.store was last built 18 July — seven days before it fell, with no code change since. osanix's last change was the 23rd, the day it peaked. The only site touched next to the cliff was tryretafit on the 24th — the one that didn't crash. Live files match the repos byte for byte.

04

We change nothing on the sites

Us

The pages are converting better since the crash — 2.63% against 1.44% before. The pages work; the top of the funnel is empty. Editing now would destroy the only clean baseline we have to measure a recovery against.

The thing that actually needs fixing

Not a bug. The shape of the business.

99.8%

of osanix.shop's traffic was one invented brand word. 98.0% for ozem-plus.store. Nobody arrives searching “best weight-loss capsules” — they search a product name we don't own, promoted with money we don't control, on a funnel we can't see.

Every site built this way has a switch, and somebody else's hand is on it. The revenue is real while it lasts, but it's rented, and the notice period is zero. tryretafit is only standing because it accidentally has a second source of traffic — and even that is borrowed from another advertiser's German TV campaign.

So the fix isn't a repair, it's a rule: no single brand word gets to be more than 60% of a site's traffic, and at least one thing we own has to earn its visitors whether or not a stranger is running ads. That's the work I'd start this week — one category site covering the whole niche instead of five sites each named after one brand that can die.
